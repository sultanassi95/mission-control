# Communicate Plainly - Specifics Over Labels

## Lesson: Never let a label carry the meaning. If a term means something, spell out the something: the concrete what, which file or step, what actually happens, and the real risk (and to whom).

**Context:** Founder feedback during product planning, after the orchestrator used a jargon shorthand ("a terms-of-service grey zone") as a stand-in for a real concern instead of just saying the concern.

**What happened:** The founder flagged it: a vague-but-loaded label signals *something* matters without saying *exactly what* - and that is precisely what erodes trust with a decision-maker. He cares about specifics.

**Transferable rule:**
- **Define every technical term in plain words the moment it appears**, inline, in the same breath.
- **Lead with plain language**, then add precision. Plain does not mean vague - be plain AND specific.
- **Banned moves:** dropping acronyms or named concepts as if understood; vague qualifiers ("grey zone," "some considerations," "edge cases," "non-trivial") used instead of the actual detail; burying the specific behind a summary word.

Worked example:
- Wrong: "Re-fetching the source is a terms-of-service grey zone."
- Right: "To cut a clip we need the video file. For a platform link, the only way to get it programmatically is to download the stream with a third-party tool - and the platform's rules forbid downloading except through their own button or API. So the creator owns the video (copyright is fine), but the download method breaks the platform's rules. Clean fix: only cut from files the creator gave us or that are already in our storage."

Vague labels feel efficient but transfer no real information. The founder is making decisions and needs the actual facts, not a flag that facts exist. Precision IS the respect.
